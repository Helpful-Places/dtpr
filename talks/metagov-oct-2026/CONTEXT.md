# Metagov seminar talk — glossary

Vocabulary for the October 2026 Metagov seminar deck. This is the talk's language, not the schema's; schema terms (category, element, datachain) keep their meaning from `api/`.

## Terms

**Maturity stage** — Where an organization *is* in its relationship to the AI it uses. An organization occupies one stage; it does not "pass through" stages the way a use case would. Contrast with *step*, which the earlier product diagram used and which we do not use here.

**In the dark** — Stage 0. The organization knows its staff use LLMs but has no picture of how. "Doesn't know what it doesn't know." Not *unaware*: awareness is present, visibility is not.

**Sensing** — Stage 1. Instruments that surface how staff actually use AI across the organization. Two exist: a one-off staff *questionnaire* (run with one organization, August 2026) and a weekly *check-in* by Slack or email (built, not yet run end to end). The talk shows both and says which produced which result.

**Governing** — Stage 2. Surfaced uses converge into named *use cases*. A use case can be drawn as a DTPR canvas before any deployed system exists; its blanks ("No safeguard in place yet", "Nothing promised yet", unvetted tools) are the policy agenda. "Living policy" is the proposed next move from there, an idea rather than a built thing, and the talk says so.

**Use case** — A named cluster of uses: drafting and editing writing, research, data and spreadsheets. The unit Governing works with. Thinner than a deployed system: the vendor is "whatever tools staff named", the operator is the organization with no guidance yet.

**Describing** — Stage 3. An internal registry describes each deployed system as a datachain. DTPR's vocabulary applies from here.

**Publishing** — Stage 4. Selected internal entries are published externally in the open standard.

**Engaging** — Stage 5. People outside read, question and respond to published entries. What they raise flows back.

**Use** (of an LLM) — Broad, fast-moving, individual activity with a general-purpose tool. Drafting, summarizing, coding. Does not fit DTPR's categories directly because it has no fixed purpose, data flow, or accountable deployment.

**Deployed system** — An AI or sensing technology with a fixed purpose and a fixed place in a process: a school-matching algorithm, a border-screening model, a license-plate camera, a customer-service chatbot. The unit DTPR describes. The Governing stage is where broad *use* converges into named use cases, and some of those become deployed systems.

**Datachain** — DTPR's record of one system or use case: what it collects, who is responsible, who is affected, and your rights. One schema covers sensors and AI (`dtpr@2026-09-01-beta`).

**DTPR's reach on the spectrum** — Faint under Governing (use-case canvases, experimental), solid from Describing onward.

**Internal registry** — The organization's own collection of datachains, visible to staff. May include entries never published.

**External registry** — The published subset, in the open standard, visible to the public. "Register" is what Clarable calls its external registry; the talk uses *registry* for the concept and *register* only when naming a Clarable page.

**Feedback track** — A loop from readers of a registry back into the organization. Two tracks: an *internal track*, where disclosure is made useful to staff themselves (how colleagues use AI, how much they trust it, where the organization's median is heading) and returns to Governing; and an *external track*, where the public reads, questions and responds and what they raise returns to Sensing. Whether they are one mechanism with two audiences or two mechanisms is an open design question the talk puts to the room.

**Public feedback** — More than a reaction score. A published datachain documents the rights a person has over a deployed system, links to actions they can take (a data protection officer, public or legal recourse), and the risks and mitigations already planned. Those are the substance of the external track; the reaction is the thin end.

**Partner organization** — The organization whose questionnaire results appear in the talk is not named on any slide. Say "a small cultural organization in Canada, 18 staff". Only the paraphrased client-build copy is used, never verbatim staff quotes.

**DTPR** — Digital Trust for Places and Routines. Said plainly as "DTPR". Not "DTPR for AI" and not "unified DTPR": there is one DTPR and it covers sensors and AI systems.
