# MakeShift 2026 — DTPR for AI plenary

**Event.** MakeShift 2026 · School of Visual Arts, NYC · 2026-05-20–21
**Slot.** DTPR for AI plenary, 30 minutes
**Speaker.** Jonathan Pichot, Helpful Places

## Talk in one sentence

> Regulators write the rules. Researchers describe the harms. Vendors document their systems. **DTPR for AI** is the visual translation layer that brings all of that into something a person on the sidewalk can read — and we just used it to render NYC's entire 2025 AI disclosure.

## Arc

A build-to-reveal plenary that climbs from the gap (NYC discloses 86 AI systems, but disclosure ≠ comprehension) to the launch (`nyc.clarable.ai/register` — the OTI 2025 AI report rendered as DTPR datachains). The constructive throughline: **build on the work of public servants, researchers, and advocacy groups already in this space.**

### ACT 3 — Research-backed, not vibes-backed (5 min, 6 slides)

**21 · "How do we decide what goes in?"** *(20s)*

- Visual: two versions of the same risk — cause framing ("opaque decision-making") vs. victim framing ("loss of autonomy")

### ACT 4 — The commons (3 min, 4 slides)

**28 · Five surfaces, one vocabulary** *(45s)*
- Visual: pentagon diagram — Schema · REST · MCP · `@dtpr/ui` · Claude plugin — with `dtpr.ai` at the center; CC-BY-4.0 stamp
- Beat: "One open vocabulary. Five ways to consume it. So nobody has an excuse to leave the disclosure trapped in a PDF."

**29 · Comprehension audit** *(45s)*
- Visual: snippet of an actual comprehension audit output from the Claude skill — rubric scores against "would a non-expert get this on a sign?"
- Beat: "Every element passes a public-comprehension rubric *before* it ships. Comprehension is a quality gate, not a hope."

**30 · Authoring provenance** *(60s)*
