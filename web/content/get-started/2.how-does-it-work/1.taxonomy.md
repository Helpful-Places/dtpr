# Taxonomy

A taxonomy is a collection of concepts that have been grouped into categories based on shared traits. For example, when scientists describe different types of animals, they don’t start from scratch every time. Rather, they ask whether it has fur, and if the answer’s yes they know it’s a mammal and so is likely also warm blooded. They ask how many legs it has, and if it’s six they know that it’s likely an insect and so it probably also has a hard outer shell. These questions help them to understand the animal they’re evaluating.

DTPR works in the same way. First, we scoured the academic literature, hosted public workshops, and talked with community members to understand their concerns around technology in their lives. What kinds of questions were they asking? What answers did they wish they had, or felt were being hidden from them? Then we grouped those questions into categories. Some of those questions are common to all types of technology:

- Who is **accountable** for this technology?
- What is the **purpose** of this technology?
- What type of data is being **collected**?
- How will this data be **processed**?
- Who can **access** this data?
- How is this data **stored**?

Others are unique, and depend on the type of technology. If it’s a physical device, such as a camera, air quality sensor, or wifi router, we use the following questions:

- What type of **technology** is this?

If it’s an AI or algorithm, such as a chat bot or a recommendation system, we use these instead:

- How does this program. **function**
- What are some **risks** of this system, and how are they **mitigated**
- What **rights** do I have in relation to this system?
- What kind of data is **input** into this system, and what kind of data does it **output**?
- What kind of **algorithm** is being used?
