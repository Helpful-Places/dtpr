Facilitation kit · v5 · disclosure poster + negotiation field

DTPR Agreement Workshop

The governing change from v4: every mechanic is a prompt, not a rule. Nothing is declared, scored, or enforced — the paper exists to structure conversation, make trade-offs visible, and give the tensions real stakes. The facilitator runs the room; the kit just keeps the trade-offs in front of everyone.

Four structural moves: disclosure becomes a peel-to-reveal poster (post-its cover the facts; the taxonomy stays visible). Surfacing runs who-then-what: a Who wall maps every group the system affects — neutral and good count — then a Futures wall pairs spark-card events with those groups to write what could happen, a lightweight red-team the room does to its own proposal. Deliberation happens on a two-axis negotiation field — trust and utility are independent, so win-wins have somewhere to go; the red line is printed geography (the shaded low bands). And the cards feed the boards: each redesign card carries its own mini-field for the arrows, while tension cards carry who a tension lands on and the event that showed it onto the mat's ledger.

Nine print pieces, in order of use. ① Run-of-show · ②a Disclosure poster (11×17) · ②b Who wall · ②c Futures wall · ③ Tension cards · ④ Negotiation mat (17×11) · ⑤ Redesign cards · ⑥ Spark deck · ⑦ Materials & facilitator crib. Every page with example content (the fare-gate case) ships with a blank twin to print for any scenario.
Open questions for the playtest: ⑴ do the redesign mini-fields want two pens (one per seat color) or one shared pen? ⑵ do the faint field ticks help (anchors) or hurt (false precision)? — coarsen to five bands if the room stalls on numbers · ⑶ should the "sealed cell holds trust down" nudge stay printed on the mat, or move to verbal facilitator guidance? · ⑷ in a small room, do the two walls collapse happily into one?

Tension cards

Written in Cluster from the futures-wall stories, played onto the mat — one card per ledger row. A tension lands on someone specific and was shown by some event — the card carries both.

How the ledger gets made
  1. At the Futures wall, cluster the stories. Give each cluster a name — that name is a tension. One card each.
  2. On the card, note who it lands on and the event that showed it. A sealed NOT DISCLOSED cell always gets a card too.
  3. Bring every card to the mat. Merge duplicates — a tension that several stories reached is telling you something.
  4. Ask the room once: "Is there a tension no story caught?" Write it now.
  5. Pick the 3–5 the room most needs to negotiate. Slot them into ledger rows and number them. The rest stay on the table, photographed but not negotiated.

Redesign cards

Played in Deliberate to answer a tension card. Pick a printed pattern or hand-write your own on a blank. Each card carries its own mini-field: before the coin moves, each seat draws the arrow it believes this move makes — deployer on utility, community on trust — and the room argues the length. The agreed move goes to the big field and the move log.

⑥ Spark deck · Letter · cut & play
DTPR · Agreement Workshop

Spark cards

These cards inspire thought; they don't test anyone. Scenario sparks are people-shapes — dealt face-up on the Who wall to seed the map. Core and pattern sparks are events — paired with a group on the Futures wall, then drawn again in Deliberate to stress-test a condition. The deck argues both ways on purpose: the upside cards are real futures, and a good negotiation prices those too. Family = the color band; carry-tier = the corner badge.

Deployer event Vendor event Community context Upside
Carry model: core — print once, every scenario · pattern — same slot, reflavor per system · scenario — write fresh with the community. The scenario cards are the deck that must be built with a neighbourhood, not about one.

The crib — lines that do work
"Questions only — positions come later."
Disclose. Protects comprehension from debate.
"Write silently first."
Both walls. Quiet voices get on the wall before loud ones.
"Close enough — the ring isn't the finding, the group is."
Map who. Don't let placement debates eat the clock.
"Nobody has to write about their own group."
Futures. The card proposes the event — answering a hypothetical, not volunteering a fear.
"What does that actually cost you? Say it to the room."
Deliberate, to the deployer, when a redesign is played. The cheap trades get exposed as cheap; the expensive ones get honestly priced.
"Where does that move trust? Your hand on the coin, not theirs."
Deliberate, to the community. Trust is theirs to grant — the deployer can argue for it, never claim it.
"The sealed cell is still up there."
Any phase. Points at the poster; nothing else needs saying.
"A split is a finding, not a failure."
Converge. The workshop's job is a true record, not a forced consensus.
Deleted from v4, on purpose: red-line cards (the red line is now the shaded low bands printed on the field) and cut-out role tokens (dot stickers do the job better and stay put for the photograph). If a seat needs to say "this crosses a line," they say it — and the coin simply doesn't leave the shaded band.
