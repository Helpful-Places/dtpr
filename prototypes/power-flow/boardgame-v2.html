Power Flow — a negotiation in five rounds

Two seats, one system. The Deployer holds the system cards — most of them face-down. The Community holds the context cards — the lived knowledge the deployer can't see. Neither hand can price the risk alone.

Three moves from v1 → v2: (1) the information is split — face-down cards are undisclosed fields, and flipping one is the disclosure; (2) the turn structure is the Bovens loop — question → account → judgment → consequences; (3) the chips are now rules, not decoration — events resolve against whatever chips are on the table. And the house rule that carries the thesis: while no Forum card is on the table, the shared endings are locked and every Deflect seeds two Backlash cards. Accountability isn't a concession — it's what makes the cooperative endings reachable.

How a round works

The Bovens loop, dealt as turns: the forum questions, the actor accounts, the world judges, consequences land.

1 · THE QUESTIONS
Community acts
Spend attention: play Questions, reveal Context, propose a Forum card.
2 · THE ACCOUNT
Deployer responds
Per question: Answer (flip the card), Deflect (Legitimacy −1, seed Backlash), or Redesign (play & pay).
3 · THE WORLD
Draw one event
One card — a Vendor squeeze, a world event, or a Backlash. It resolves against the chips actually on the table.
4 · CONSEQUENCES
Dials move
Adjust Utility & Legitimacy. The launch deadline ticks down.

Two dials, both with floors

Utility: does the thing work. Legitimacy: will people accept it. Either one hitting the red floor ends the game for both.

Four endings — seat a Forum card below to unlock the shared ones

Both can win. Either can win alone. Both can lose. Which endings are reachable depends on what gets built at the table.

Deployed & Trusted
Utility ≥7 and Legitimacy ≥7, a Forum card in play. Both win.
Locked — no forum on the table
Redesigned & Trusted
Both ≥5, two-plus Redesigns, a Forum card in play. Both win — the deployer paid for it. The good ending costs.
Locked — no forum on the table
Deployed Over Objection
Utility ≥5, Legitimacy below 6. Deployer scores now, minus every Backlash still in the deck. See you next election.
Collapse
Either dial ≤3. Project dead — and the community loses the service too. With a Sunset Clause: wound down, both recover +2.

Question deck · 9 cards

community plays

The forum poses questions — one each. The card's shape badge shows which system card it aims at. Every question resolves the same way: Answer (flip it, free — but now it's public), Deflect (Legitimacy −1, seed a Backlash), or Redesign (play one and pay). This whole deck carries to every scenario verbatim.

Forum deck · 6 cards

community proposes · deployer funds

The rule-changers — each one is an accountable_to edge made physical. The community proposes ( 1), the deployer funds ( printed cost): both must touch it, that's the negotiation. Each unlocks a community move and absorbs event damage for the deployer — insurance, not concession. Click one to seat it at the table and watch the endings unlock.

Redesign deck · 7 cards

deployer plays

The design space — proof the win-win region exists. Each swaps a card or chip on the table, at a price. The patterns (move it on-device, purge sooner, opt-out lane, human in the loop, warrant gate…) recur in every scenario; the flavor is this scenario's.

Event deck · 8 world + 5 vendor + 4 Backlash · one draw / round

the world moves

Reality, shuffled — one card per round at The World. Most resolve against the board: the same breach is a catastrophe with "7 years, vendor cloud" and a shrug with "24 hours, on-device." Vendor cards (blue) are FaceGate squeezing mid-chain — the deployer discovers they don't fully control their own system either, which keeps them from being the cartoon villain. Backlash cards (dark) enter the deck when the deployer deflects or steamrolls: unilateral power works, and the costs arrive later, compounding. Playtest knob: the vendor:world ratio sets how often the squeeze lands — dial it up (or seed early rounds) if vendor pressure should feel constant rather than occasional.

What carries over between scenarios

Core — travels verbatim

The engine. Print once, play every scenario.

  • All 9 Question cards — they interrogate categories, not systems
  • All 6 Forum cards — rights are portable by design
  • 4 core Events — breach, budget cut, election year, the FOIA
  • 2 core Backlash — court, council hearing
  • Turn structure, dials, endings, the zero-sum lock rule

Pattern — slot recurs, text reflavors

Templates with scenario skin.

  • System cards — same 9 slots (pitch, model, reads, makes, lives, sees, long, fine print, sealed), new contents
  • The Sealed card — every scenario has one thing nobody at the table knows
  • Redesign patterns — on-device / purge / opt-out / human-in-loop / warrant recur; costs and side-effects reflavor
  • 2 Backlash — the organized-refusal and quiet-noncompliance slots

Scenario — written fresh

Where the writing effort goes per deck.

  • The brief — premise, budgets, starting dials
  • All 7 Context cards — lived knowledge is never generic; this is the deck that must be written with a community, not about one
  • All 5 Vendor cards — each vendor squeezes differently; they now live in the Event deck, one draw among many
  • 4 scenario Events — the concrete failures only this system can have
Not a 1:1 element mapping, on purpose — cards borrow DTPR where it earns its place: category shapes and names plate the system cards; chips are the real discriminators (autonomy, pii, and the new affected.relationship from ai@2026-08-12-beta on the context cards); question cards aim at categories; forum cards are the rights elements (right_to_notice, right_to_human_review, right_contest…) turned into rule-changers; icons are the schema's own symbols/. The community's chair is the affected category made physical; seating a forum card is drawing the accountable_to edge from the research entry. End of a session, photograph the table: the system cards are a datachain, the seated forum cards are its edges. Untuned by design: every number here is a playtest question, not an answer.
