# Relational & graph models for AI power/accountability disclosure

Prior art for reshaping a DTPR datachain from a **flat list of categories** into a **typed relationship graph** ("power flow") that renders *who does what to whom* — the Doctorow "done FOR vs. done TO" lens. Synthesizes four threads: (1) accountability theory that says accountability *is a relation, not a property*; (2) standards-body typed-relation data models to borrow the grammar from; (3) stakeholder/power-mapping methods that already draw directed power; (4) comprehension evidence on graphs-vs-lists for lay audiences. Captured against schema `ai@2026-05-06-beta`.

## Why this matters for DTPR

The `accountable` category (required; `role` discriminator ∈ {vendor, deployer}) records *that* an org is answerable but not *to whom*. The peer-reviewed literature calls this exact move a failure: accountability is definitionally a **relationship between an actor and a forum**, and publishing an account while dropping the forum is a "grammatical illusion" of accountability. A flat category list can name parties (nodes) but structurally cannot express the relationships (edges) where power lives. This entry grounds a **typed directed-edge overlay** — additive on today's element instances, with the linear chain kept as the default glanceable read and the graph as an opt-in deep view.

## Thread 1 — Accountability is a relation, not a property (the warrant for an `accountable_to` edge)

- **Bovens (2007)** — canonical definition, quoted verbatim: accountability is *"a relationship between an actor and a forum, in which the actor has an obligation to explain and to justify his or her conduct, the forum can pose questions and pass judgement, and the actor may face consequences."* Six constitutive elements: **actor**, **forum** (the *to whom* — a specific person, agency, or "any group who believe an actor has an obligation to account to them"), **conduct/relationship**, **obligation to explain & justify**, **forum questions + judges**, **consequences**. Bovens frames analysis as *who is accountable, for what, **to whom**, under which circumstances* — the "to whom" is a first-class parameter. Distinguishes accountability-as-**virtue** (a property one has) from accountability-as-**mechanism** (a relationship one is in); the mechanism sense yields the actor→forum edge. `[peer-reviewed]` https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0386.2007.00378.x (OA: https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/35005)
- **Wieringa (2020), "What to account for when accounting for algorithms"** — FAccT systematic review (242 articles, PRISMA) that ports Bovens' actor–forum model onto algorithms; defines algorithmic accountability as "a networked account for a sociotechnical algorithmic system" across the lifecycle. Best Student Paper. `[peer-reviewed]` https://doi.org/10.1145/3351095.3372833
- **Cooper, Moss, Laufer & Nissenbaum (2022), "Accountability in an Algorithmic Society"** — FAccT; "accountability exists when an accountable actor has an enforceable obligation to a forum"; operationalizes who / for what / **to whom** / under which circumstances, and names the *to-whom/forum* as the underdeveloped parameter (forum = "those who are themselves harmed," their representatives, journalists, officials, "the many publics"). `[peer-reviewed]` https://doi.org/10.1145/3531146.3533150
- **Metcalf, Moss, Singh, Tafese & Watkins (2023), "A relationship and not a thing"** — Data & Society / FAccT; the keystone. §2 heading: **"ACCOUNTABILITY IS A RELATION, NOT A PROPERTY."** "accountability resides in the relations between the developers, regulators, and public … not in the algorithmic system nor in the developer's practices alone." Names the **"grammatical illusion"** — treating "merely exposing an account of how an algorithm works" as satisfying accountability. "If there is no formal method by which people affected by algorithmic systems might demand changes … then 'accountability' is not meaningful." Proposes a **triadic** relation (actor → forum → public), not just a dyad. `[peer-reviewed]` https://doi.org/10.1145/3593013.3593178
- **Impact vs. accountability are distinct edges.** Whom a system *impacts* (Bovens' "for what"; the harm relation `actor → affects → affected-public`) and whom an operator is *answerable to* (Bovens' "to whom"; `actor → accountable-to → forum`) are separate directed edges that **need not share endpoints** — a system can affect commuters while the operator answers only to a regulator. Surfacing that gap (**affected-but-no-forum**) is the transparency payoff and can be made machine-visible. Contrast: Kroll et al. "Accountable Algorithms" (2017, *U. Penn. L. Rev.* 165(3):633–705, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2765268) and Diakopoulos (2015) sit on the virtue/technical-property side and under-specify the forum. `[peer-reviewed / law-review]`

## Thread 2 — Typed-relation data models (borrow the grammar, not the presentation)

- **W3C PROV** (PROV-DM + PROV-O, **W3C Recommendation, 30 Apr 2013**; eds. Moreau & Missier). Three types: **Entity / Activity / Agent**. Seven starting-point directed relations: `wasGeneratedBy` · `used` · `wasInformedBy` · `wasDerivedFrom` · `wasAttributedTo` · `wasAssociatedWith` · `actedOnBehalfOf`. **Key transferable idea: endpoint-typing** — each relation is constrained by the types of its endpoints (`wasAttributedTo`: Entity→Agent; `actedOnBehalfOf`: Agent→Agent; `used`: Activity→Entity). This is the domain/range discipline for a DTPR `relations` vocabulary. Used in practice for ML lineage (PROV-ML, ProvLake, MLflow2PROV), GDPR data-protection provenance, FAIR data. `[standards-body]` https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/ · https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/
- **PROV is NOT lay-readable** — and this is the load-bearing caution. Non-experts **misread arrow direction** (PROV edges encode *dependency*, read intuitively as forward data-flow) — "Provenance for the People," HCI study (ResearchGate 403 on direct fetch; captured from index). Corroborated by Simmons et al. (2018, arXiv:1812.05804): PROV "terminology is unlikely to match practitioners'," notations "suffer from ineffective use of visual design space," readability "unlikely to persist for larger, more complex graphs." Borrow PROV's typed-relation grammar; never show PROV to a commuter. `[peer-reviewed]`
- **W3C ODRL 2.2** (**W3C Recommendation, 15 Feb 2018**) — deontic policy language expressing **permissions / prohibitions / duties** binding Parties to Actions. The natural formalism for a **deontic edge class** DTPR needs — `affected → can → right` (and future `deployer → must-not → …`) — which plain PROV lacks. `[standards-body]` https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/
- **schema.org** (typed entities + typed properties over a controlled type hierarchy; the dominant web-metadata vocabulary) and **RDF** (subject–predicate–object triples) are the generic "typed directed edge over named nodes" substrate; **C2PA / Content Credentials** (v2.1, Sept 2024, added AI-training-data disclosure) is the artifact-level provenance complement. Adopt a triple mental model so a DTPR graph can export to any of these later. `[official-org / standards-body]` https://schema.org/ · https://spec.c2pa.org/

## Thread 3 — Stakeholder / power mapping (how directed power gets drawn)

- **Net-Map** (Schiffer & Hauck 2010, *Field Methods* 22(3):231–249; IFPRI) — the strongest lay-facing template: actors = nodes, **directed color-coded arrows** = relationship types (money, information, command, support), and a stacked **"influence tower"** per actor encodes *magnitude* of power on the issue. Renders direction + type + magnitude on one issue-scoped picture. Adds a dimension worth considering: **edge magnitude**, not just existence. `[peer-reviewed]` https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1525822X10374798
- **RACI** — external precedent for splitting `operates` vs. `accountable_to`: **Responsible** (the doer) vs. **Accountable** (the single party answerable for the outcome). Convention: **exactly one Accountable per decision** — a legibility constraint that stops a power graph sprouting diffuse, unownable accountability. `[textbook / PMBOK]` https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility_assignment_matrix
- **Mendelow power–interest grid** (1991) — 2-axis placement (power × interest), 4 quadrants (manage closely / keep satisfied / keep informed / monitor). Teaches restraint, but places actors *independently* — **no edges** — which is exactly why a lay power map needs relationships, not just positions. `[textbook]`
- **Data Feminism** (D'Ignazio & Klein 2020, MIT Press, open access) — "Power Chapter"; Collins' **matrix of domination** (structural / disciplinary / hegemonic / interpersonal). Framing lens for *naming who holds power and who is affected*. Civic anchors `[practitioner]`: **Our Data Bodies** (Digital Defense Playbook); **Detroit Community Technology Project — Data Justice** (mapped Project Green Light surveillance cameras onto a public neighborhood map). https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu/ · https://detroitcommunitytech.org/datajustice
- **Caveat:** these are mostly **facilitator/analyst tools**, not lay-reader artifacts — value comes from *building* the map together; outputs clutter fast (Schröter et al. 2018, *Research Evaluation*, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2059799118787754). For a commuter you must do the reduction facilitators do live: cap node count, expose a tiny fixed legend of edge types, use concrete verbs, anchor magnitude cues. `[peer-reviewed]`

## Thread 4 — Graph-vs-list comprehension (why the chain stays the default)

- **Ghoniem, Fekete & Castagliola (2004/2005)** — matrices beat node-link on nearly every task once graphs exceed **~20 vertices**; node-link reliably wins **only path-finding** (tracing a route), and even that fades at high node counts. Implication: node-link's one strength is following a chain — which a *linear chain already gives for free*. Design consequence: shape a `power_flow` as a **short directed path** (e.g. camera → vendor → police; deployer → affects → riders), not a dense mesh. `[peer-reviewed]` http://www-sop.inria.fr/orion/COGC/teams/INSITUghoniem-fivj05-final.pdf
- **Galesic & Garcia-Retamero (2011)** — ~**one-third of adults** have low graph literacy even for simple bar/line charts (representative US/DE samples). A node-edge diagram is far more demanding; a general commuter audience cannot be assumed to decode it. `[peer-reviewed]` https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0272989x10373805
- **Edges/arrows are systematically misread** — a documented **reification** tendency (treat an arrow as a literal "A causes/controls B" mechanism) among experts *and* non-experts. Second independent line of caution beyond PROV's arrow-direction problem. `[peer-reviewed]` https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7064555/
- **Every mature AI-transparency artifact chose structured fields, not graphs** — Model Cards (Mitchell 2019), Datasheets for Datasets (Gebru 2018; 57 questions/7 categories), Amsterdam/Helsinki AI registers (2020), Eurocities Algorithmic Transparency Standard (CSV/Excel/JSON schema), **UK ATRS** — the stated reason is repeatedly **comparability + lay accessibility**. A bespoke graph-per-system destroys glance-and-compare. `[official-org / peer-reviewed]`
- **Dual-view / progressive disclosure has real (if bounded) support** — the privacy **"nutrition label" RCT** (Kelley et al., CMU 2009, n=789) found a standardized table with a **small fixed symbol vocabulary** beat both free text *and* layered text on accuracy/speed (lineage → Apple App Store privacy labels 2020). DTPR's chain is already this format. Layered privacy notices (Art. 29 WP / ICO) and **UK ATRS Tier 1 (plain-language summary) / Tier 2 (detailed fields)** are live precedents for *glanceable default + optional deep view* — the graph belongs at Tier-2 depth, never primary. **Honest caveat:** general HCI support for progressive disclosure is thinner than assumed (strongest from privacy-notice/nutrition-label studies specifically); usability-test with the actual commuter audience. `[peer-reviewed / official-org]` http://cups.cs.cmu.edu/privacyLabel/

## Application to DTPR

1. **Model the power layer as a typed directed-edge overlay**, additive on existing element instances: type-level `relations` vocabulary (with PROV-style endpoint domain/range constraints) + instance-level `edges: [{from, to, relation}]` defaulting to `[]` (zero edges = today exactly; fully backward compatible; no category retirement).
2. **Keep `affects` and `accountable_to` as separate relations** (Bovens/Metcalf warrant); make the **accountability gap** (system affects the public but answers only to vendor/regulator) machine-visible — a distinctive transparency payoff.
3. **Split off a deontic edge class** (`can` / future `must-not`) per ODRL to ground the otherwise-floating `rights` elements to the affected party.
4. **Shape `power_flow` as a short path, not a mesh** (Ghoniem): few nodes, low edge-crossing — the one regime where node-link helps laypeople.
5. **Chain stays the default glanceable, comparable layer; graph is opt-in Tier-2** (UK ATRS, nutrition-label RCT). Small fixed vocabulary, concrete verb labels ("run by", "used on", "answers to", "you can"), anchored magnitude if borrowed from Net-Map. Avoid PROV/arrow presentation.
6. **Prerequisite:** an **affected node** (the forum/subject) must exist for the edges to point at — either a third `role` on a reframed `power_dynamics` category or a standalone `affected` category.
